  <h2>What Is an Annuity Calculator?</h2>
 <p>An <strong>annuity calculator</strong>is a financial tool used to calculate the present value (PV), future value (FV), or periodic payment (PMT) of a series of equal payments. It helps you understand how regular deposits or payments change in value over time when interest is applied. </p><p>The free EasyCalculator.org Annuity Calculator supports both <strong>ordinary annuities</strong>and <strong>annuities due</strong>. You can use it for retirement savings estimates, recurring investments, education funds, and financial planning. </p><h2>How to Use the Annuity Calculator</h2><ol><li><strong>Select a calculation:</strong>Choose Future Value (FV), Present Value (PV), or Payment (PMT). </li><li><strong>Choose the annuity type:</strong>Select Ordinary Annuity for end-of-period payments or Annuity Due for beginning-of-period payments. </li><li><strong>Select your currency:</strong>Choose USD, INR, EUR, GBP, CAD, or AUD. </li><li><strong>Enter payment details:</strong>Provide the payment per period, interest rate per period, and number of periods. </li><li><strong>Set a target:</strong>In PMT mode, enter the future value you want to achieve. </li><li><strong>Click Calculate:</strong>View the calculated PV, FV, PMT, total payments, and interest or growth. </li></ol><div class="alert alert-info"><strong>Important:</strong>Your interest rate and payment periods must use the same frequency. For example, when calculating monthly payments, use a monthly interest rate and the total number of months. </div><h2>Understanding PV, FV, and PMT</h2><div class="table-responsive"><table class="table table-bordered table-striped"><thead><tr><th>Term</th><th>Meaning</th><th>Purpose</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>PV</td><td>Present Value</td><td>Value today of a series of future payments. </td></tr><tr><td>FV</td><td>Future Value</td><td>Accumulated value of payments at the end of the investment period. </td></tr><tr><td>PMT</td><td>Periodic Payment</td><td>Amount to contribute each period to reach a target future value. </td></tr><tr><td>r</td><td>Interest Rate per Period</td><td>Interest rate expressed as a decimal. </td></tr><tr><td>n</td><td>Number of Periods</td><td>Total number of scheduled payments. </td></tr></tbody></table></div><h2>Annuity Calculator Formulas</h2><p>The following formulas apply to an <strong>ordinary annuity</strong>, where equal payments are made at the end of every period and the periodic interest rate is greater than zero. </p><h3>1. Future Value (FV) Formula</h3><p>Future value estimates how much a series of regular payments will accumulate to over time. </p><div class="bg-light border rounded p-3 my-3"><code>FV=PMT &times; [((1 + r)<sup>n</sup>- 1) / r] </code></div><p>This formula is useful when estimating the future balance of recurring savings or investment contributions. </p><h3>2. Present Value (PV) Formula</h3><p>Present value calculates how much future payments are worth today after discounting them using the specified interest rate. </p><div class="bg-light border rounded p-3 my-3"><code>PV=PMT &times; [(1 - (1 + r)<sup>-n</sup>) / r] </code></div><p>This calculation is commonly used to value a series of scheduled income payments or financial obligations. </p><h3>3. Periodic Payment (PMT) Formula</h3><p>If you know the future amount you want to accumulate, the PMT formula determines the periodic contribution required to reach that goal. </p><div class="bg-light border rounded p-3 my-3"><code>PMT=FV &times; [r / ((1 + r)<sup>n</sup>- 1)] </code></div><p>The payment calculation in this calculator uses a <strong>target future value</strong>and assumes equal contributions throughout the selected period. </p><h3>Annuity Due Formula Adjustment</h3><p>For an annuity due, payments occur at the beginning of each period and therefore earn interest for one additional period. </p><div class="bg-light border rounded p-3 my-3"><code>FV (Due)=FV (Ordinary) &times; (1 + r) </code><hr><code>PV (Due)=PV (Ordinary) &times; (1 + r) </code></div><p>To calculate the payment required for a target future value under an annuity due, divide the ordinary annuity payment by (1 + r). When the interest rate is zero, both annuity types use FV=PV=PMT &times; n. </p><h2>Annuity Calculation Example</h2><p>Suppose you deposit <strong>$200 every month</strong>for 10 years and earn an assumed annual interest rate of 6%, compounded monthly. </p><div class="table-responsive"><table class="table table-bordered"><tbody><tr><th>Monthly Payment</th><td>$200</td></tr><tr><th>Annual Interest Rate</th><td>6%</td></tr><tr><th>Monthly Interest Rate</th><td>6% / 12=0.5%</td></tr><tr><th>Investment Duration</th><td>10 years</td></tr><tr><th>Number of Periods</th><td>10 &times; 12=120 months</td></tr><tr><th>Annuity Type</th><td>Ordinary Annuity</td></tr></tbody></table></div><h3>Step 1: Calculate Future Value</h3><p>Using the ordinary annuity future value formula: </p><div class="bg-light border rounded p-3 my-3"><p class="mb-2">FV=200 &times; [((1.005)<sup>120</sup>- 1) / 0.005] </p><p class="fw-bold fs-5 mb-0">Future Value=$32,775.87 </p></div><h3>Step 2: Calculate Total Contributions</h3><p>Total Contributions=$200 &times; 120=<strong>$24,000</strong>. </p><h3>Step 3: Calculate Interest Earned</h3><p>Estimated Interest=$32,775.87 - $24,000=<strong>$8,775.87</strong>. </p><div class="alert alert-success"><strong>Result:</strong>By contributing $200 every month for 10 years at a constant 6% annual rate compounded monthly, your estimated final balance is <strong>$32,775.87</strong>. If payments are made at the beginning of each month, the estimated future value increases to <strong>$32,939.75</strong>. </div><h2>Ordinary Annuity vs. Annuity Due</h2><p>The primary difference between these two types of annuities is when the payment occurs. </p><div class="table-responsive"><table class="table table-bordered table-striped"><thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Ordinary Annuity</th><th>Annuity Due</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Payment Timing</td><td>End of period</td><td>Beginning of period</td></tr><tr><td>Compounding</td><td>Less time for growth</td><td>One additional period</td></tr><tr><td>Future Value</td><td>Generally lower</td><td>Generally higher</td></tr><tr><td>Example</td><td>Payments made at month-end</td><td>Payments made at month-start</td></tr></tbody></table></div><h2>Where Is an Annuity Calculator Useful?</h2><p>Annuity calculations can support several types of personal and financial planning: </p><ul><li><strong>Retirement planning:</strong>Estimate the accumulated value of regular retirement savings contributions. </li><li><strong>Investment planning:</strong>Estimate the future value of recurring deposits under an assumed fixed rate. </li><li><strong>Education savings:</strong>Find the monthly amount required to accumulate a target education fund. </li><li><strong>Income valuation:</strong>Estimate today's value of equal future payments. </li><li><strong>Savings goals:</strong>Work out the periodic contributions needed for a future financial target. </li></ul><h2>Important Limitations</h2><p>The calculator assumes equal payment amounts, a fixed interest rate per period, and a specified number of payment periods. Actual investment returns may vary, and market-linked investments do not provide guaranteed returns. </p><p>Calculations do not automatically account for inflation, taxes, investment fees, or changing interest rates. Selecting a different currency changes the displayed currency; it does not convert amounts using foreign exchange rates. </p>
