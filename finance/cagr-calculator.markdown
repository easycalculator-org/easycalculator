 <!-- Introduction -->
 <div class="article-section">
  <h2>What Is CAGR?</h2>
  <p><strong>CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)</strong> is a measure of the average annual growth rate of an investment  over a specific period. It converts the growth between an initial value and a final value into an annualized rate, assuming the growth was compounded at a constant rate. </p>
  <p>CAGR is useful when you want to compare the long-term growth of investments, businesses, portfolios, or other assets over different periods. </p>
  <div class="article-note"><strong>Important:</strong> CAGR is a smoothed annual growth rate. It does not mean that the investment actually grew by the same percentage every year.</div>
  </div>
<!-- Formula -->
  <div class="article-section">
    <h2>CAGR Formula</h2>
     <p>The standard formula for calculating CAGR is: </p>
      <div class="formula-box">CAGR =  <span> (Final Value / Initial Value) <sup>1 / Number of Years</sup>  - 1 </span></div>
        <p>Where:</p>
        <div class="definition-list">
           <div><strong>Initial Value:</strong> Value of the investment at the beginning.</div>
            <div><strong>Final Value:</strong> Value of the investment at the end.</div>
            <div><strong>Number of Years:</strong> Total investment period in years.</div>
        </div>
    </div>
  <!-- How to Calculate -->
  <div class="article-section">
   <h2>How to Calculate CAGR</h2>
    <p>To calculate CAGR manually, you only need three values:  the initial investment, final investment value, and the investment period.</p>
     <ol class="steps-list">
      <li><strong>Enter the initial value.</strong> This is the amount invested at the beginning.</li>
      <li><strong>Enter the final value.</strong> This is the value of the investment at the end.</li>
      <li><strong>Enter the investment period.</strong> Enter the number of years between the two values.</li>
      <li><strong>Apply the CAGR formula.</strong> Divide the final value by the initial value, raise the  result to the power of 1 divided by the number of years,  and subtract 1.</li>
     </ol>
     <p>You can also enter these values in the calculator above to calculate CAGR instantly. </p>
    </div>
    <!-- Example -->
    <div class="article-section">
        <h2>CAGR Example</h2>
        <p>Suppose you invested <strong>₹1,00,000</strong> and the investment grew to <strong>₹2,50,000</strong> after <strong>6 years</strong>.</p>
        <div class="example-box">
            <div class="example-row">
                <span>Initial Value</span>
                <strong>₹1,00,000</strong>
            </div>
            <div class="example-row">
                <span>Final Value</span>
                <strong>₹2,50,000</strong>
            </div>
            <div class="example-row">
                <span>Investment Period</span>
                <strong>6 years</strong>
            </div>
        </div>
        <div class="calculation-box">
            <strong>Calculation:</strong>
            <div class="formula-inline">
                CAGR = (2,50,000 / 1,00,000)<sup>1/6</sup> − 1
            </div>
            <div class="calculation-result">
                CAGR = <strong>16.50%</strong>
            </div>
        </div>
        <p>This means the investment's start-to-end growth is equivalent  to a compounded annual growth rate of approximately  <strong>16.50%</strong>. </p>
    </div>
    <!-- What CAGR Means -->
    <div class="article-section">
        <h2>What Does CAGR Tell You?</h2>
        <p>CAGR provides a simple way to understand long-term growth without focusing on the fluctuations that occurred during  individual years.</p>
        <div class="info-grid">
            <div class="info-item">
                <strong>Annualized Growth</strong>
                <p> Converts total growth into an annual compounded rate. </p>
            </div>
            <div class="info-item">
                <strong>Easy Comparison</strong>
                <p>Helps compare investments with different starting  values or time periods.</p>
            </div>
            <div class="info-item">
                <strong>Long-Term View</strong>
                <p>Useful for understanding the overall growth of an investment over several years.</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </div>

    <div class="article-section">
        <h2>CAGR vs Other Return Metrics</h2>
        <p>CAGR is not the only way to measure investment performance. The right metric depends on the type of investment and  cash-flow pattern.</p>
        <div class="table-responsive">
            <table class="table comparison-table">

    <!-- Uses -->
    <div class="article-section">
      <h2>When Should You Use CAGR?</h2>
       <p> CAGR is particularly useful when you want to evaluate  long-term growth between two points in time.</p>
        <ul class="clean-list">
            <li><strong>Mutual Funds:</strong>  Compare long-term growth between investments.</li>
            <li><strong>Stocks:</strong>  Measure the annualized growth of a stock over several years.</li>
            <li><strong>Business Revenue:</strong> Measure how quickly revenue has grown over a period.</li>
            <li><strong>Real Estate:</strong> Estimate annualized property value growth.</li>
            <li><strong>Portfolio Analysis:</strong> Compare the historical growth of different portfolios.</li>
        </ul>
    </div>
    <!-- Limitations -->
    <div class="article-section">
        <h2>Limitations of CAGR</h2>
        <p> CAGR is useful, but it does not provide a complete picture of investment performance.</p>
        <ul class="clean-list">
            <li>It does not show yearly gains or losses.</li>
            <li>It assumes a smooth compounded growth rate.</li>
            <li>It does not account for intermediate deposits or withdrawals.</li>
            <li>It is not the appropriate measure for investments with multiple irregular cash flows. </li>
        </ul>
        <div class="article-note">
            <strong>For SIPs and irregular cash flows:</strong>
            XIRR is generally more appropriate because it considers the
            timing of individual cash flows.
        </div>
    </div>
    <!-- Excel -->
    <div class="article-section">
        <h2>How to Calculate CAGR in Excel</h2>
        <p>
            You can calculate CAGR in Excel using the same mathematical
            formula used by this calculator.
        </p>
        <div class="code-box">
            =((Final_Value/Initial_Value)^(1/Years))-1
        </div>
        <p>
            For example, if the initial value is in cell
            <strong>A2</strong>, the final value is in
            <strong>B2</strong>, and the number of years is in
            <strong>C2</strong>, use:
        </p>
        <div class="code-box">
            =((B2/A2)^(1/C2))-1
        </div>
        <p>Format the result cell as a percentage to display the CAGR clearly.
        </p>
    </div>

                CAGR is calculated using the initial value, final value,
                and investment period. The formula is:
                <strong>(Final Value / Initial Value)<sup>1 / Years</sup> − 1</strong>.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light">
        <div class="card-body">
            <div class="fw-bold text-primary">
                3. Is CAGR the same as annual return?
            </div>
            <p class="mb-0">
                No. CAGR represents a compounded annualized growth rate
                between two values. It does not show the actual return
                achieved in each individual year.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light">
        <div class="card-body">
            <div class="fw-bold text-primary">
                4. Can CAGR be negative?
            </div>
            <p class="mb-0">
                Yes. If the final value of an investment is lower than
                the initial value, the calculated CAGR will be negative.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light">
        <div class="card-body">
            <div class="fw-bold text-primary">
                5. Can CAGR be used for SIP investments?
            </div>
            <p class="mb-0">
                CAGR is generally suitable for a single initial investment.
                For SIPs or investments with multiple cash flows,
                XIRR is usually more appropriate because it considers
                the timing of individual cash flows.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light">
        <div class="card-body">
            <div class="fw-bold text-primary">
                6. What is the difference between CAGR and absolute return?
            </div>
            <p class="mb-0">
                Absolute return measures the total percentage gain or loss,
                while CAGR converts the growth between the starting and
                ending values into an annual compounded growth rate.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
