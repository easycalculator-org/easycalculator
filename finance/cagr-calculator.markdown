</div><h2>What is CAGR?</h2><p>CAGR means <strong>Compound Annual Growth Rate</strong>. It describes the steady yearly rate that would take an investment from its initial value to its final value over a chosen period, assuming the growth compounds each year.</p><p>For example, if an investment grows from $10,000 to $15,000 over five years, its CAGR is about 8.45% per year. This is a smoothed rate for comparing start and end values; it does not mean the investment earned exactly 8.45% in every year.</p><h2>CAGR formula</h2><div class="cg-formula" aria-label="CAGR equals the final value divided by the initial value, raised to one divided by the number of years, minus one">CAGR=(Final Value ÷ Initial Value)<sup>1 ÷ Years</sup>− 1</div><p>Multiply the result by 100 to express CAGR as a percentage. The calculator uses the same formula and formats the displayed result to two decimal places.</p><h2>How to use this CAGR calculator</h2><ol><li>Enter the investment's initial value.</li><li>Enter its final value after the period. A lower final value produces a negative CAGR.</li><li>Enter the elapsed period in years. You can use a decimal for a partial year.</li><li>Select a display currency, then choose <strong>Calculate CAGR</strong>.</li><li>Review the annualized rate, total growth, chart, and yearly table. Choose <strong>Download PDF report</strong>to save a copy.</li></ol><h2>Understanding the result</h2><p><strong>Annualized growth rate</strong>is the compounded yearly rate between the two values. <strong>Total growth</strong>is the difference between final and initial value. <strong>Total return</strong>is that difference expressed as a percentage of the initial value.</p><p>The chart and yearly table apply the CAGR evenly across the period so the start-to-end path is easy to understand. They are illustrative, not a record of actual annual performance.</p><h2>When is CAGR useful?</h2><p>CAGR is useful for comparing two investments, business revenue, property values, or portfolio balances measured at the start and end of a period. It puts different time periods on an annualized basis.</p><h2>Limits of CAGR</h2><ul><li>It hides changes and volatility between the first and last values.</li><li>It does not include fees, taxes, or inflation unless you adjust the input values yourself.</li><li>It assumes one initial amount and does not account for cash added or withdrawn during the period.</li></ul><p>For investments with regular or irregular cash flows, use an internal rate of return calculation such as <a href="/xirr-calculator">XIRR</a>, which considers when each cash flow occurs.</p><section class="p-4"><h2 class="mb-4">FAQ on CAGR</h2><div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light"><div class="card-body"><div class="fw-bold text-primary">1. What is CAGR in simple words? </div><p class="mb-0">CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) is the annualized compounded growth rate of an investment between its starting and ending values over a specific period. </p></div></div><div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light"><div class="card-body"><div class="fw-bold text-primary">2. How is CAGR calculated? </div><p class="mb-0">CAGR is calculated using the initial value, final value, and investment period. The formula is: <strong>(Final Value / Initial Value)<sup>1 / Years</sup>− 1</strong>. </p></div></div><div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light"><div class="card-body"><div class="fw-bold text-primary">3. Is CAGR the same as annual return? </div><p class="mb-0">No. CAGR represents a compounded annualized growth rate between two values. It does not show the actual return achieved in each individual year. </p></div></div><div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light"><div class="card-body"><div class="fw-bold text-primary">4. Can CAGR be negative? </div><p class="mb-0">Yes. If the final value of an investment is lower than the initial value, the calculated CAGR will be negative. </p></div></div><div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light"><div class="card-body"><div class="fw-bold text-primary">5. Can CAGR be used for SIP investments? </div><p class="mb-0">CAGR is generally suitable for a single initial investment. For SIPs or investments with multiple cash flows, XIRR is usually more appropriate because it considers the timing of individual cash flows. </p></div></div><div class="card mb-3 border-0 bg-light"><div class="card-body"><div class="fw-bold text-primary">6. What is the difference between CAGR and absolute return?</div><p class="mb-0">Absolute return measures the total percentage gain or loss, while CAGR converts the growth between the starting and ending values into an annual compounded growth rate.</p></div></div></section></article></main>
