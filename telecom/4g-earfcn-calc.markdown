 <h2>What is an LTE EARFCN?</h2><p>EARFCN stands for <strong>E-UTRA Absolute Radio Frequency Channel Number</strong>. It is the channel number used to describe an LTE carrier frequency. Engineers use it when configuring cells, checking drive-test logs and comparing network frequencies.</p><p>A band groups a range of radio frequencies. Each band has its own starting frequency and EARFCN offset. That is why the calculator checks both the channel number and the selected link direction.</p>
<div class="p-4 bg-light"><h2>How to use this calculator</h2><ol><li>Choose <strong>EARFCN → Frequency</strong> or <strong>Frequency → EARFCN</strong>.</li><li>Select an LTE band, or use <strong>Auto-detect</strong> to list every match.</li><li>Choose downlink or uplink, enter your value, and press <strong>Calculate</strong>.</li><li>Review the frequency, channel number, band range and relevant notes. Copy the result or download all matches as CSV.</li></ol><p>When several bands cover the same frequency, auto-detect shows multiple answers. Select the band used by your network to narrow the result.</p></div>
<h2>EARFCN to frequency formula</h2><div class="le-formula">Frequency (MHz) = F<sub>low</sub> + 0.1 × (EARFCN − N<sub>offset</sub>)<br>EARFCN = N<sub>offset</sub> + 10 × (Frequency in MHz − F<sub>low</sub>)</div><p>The calculator uses the appropriate downlink or uplink starting frequency and offset. One channel-number step changes the mapped frequency by <strong>0.1 MHz (100 kHz)</strong>. A frequency between raster points has no exact integer EARFCN; this calculator rejects it instead of silently rounding.</p>
<h3>Example: Band 3 EARFCN 1650</h3><p>Band 3 downlink starts at 1805 MHz with offset 1200. The calculation is <strong>1805 + 0.1 × (1650 − 1200) = 1850 MHz</strong>. Reversing the calculation gives <strong>1200 + 10 × (1850 − 1805) = 1650</strong>. The corresponding conventional Band 3 uplink channel is 19650 at 1755 MHz.</p>
<h2>FDD, TDD and downlink-only bands</h2><ul><li><strong>FDD:</strong> uplink and downlink occupy separate frequency ranges. Band 7 is FDD.</li><li><strong>TDD:</strong> uplink and downlink share a frequency range and transmit at different times. Bands 40 and 41 are examples.</li><li><strong>SDL:</strong> supplemental downlink has no uplink channel in that band and requires an appropriate carrier-aggregation configuration.</li><li><strong>Special use:</strong> Band 47 is associated with V2X; Bands 107 and 108 are LTE-based terrestrial broadcast bands. These are identified separately from ordinary mobile broadband use.</li></ul>

<h2>Channel mapping versus a usable carrier</h2><p>A valid EARFCN mapping does not automatically make a deployable channel. The carrier bandwidth must fit within the operating band. Band-specific bandwidths, carrier aggregation, equipment capabilities and local spectrum permissions also matter.</p><p>Band 46 has additional channel restrictions. Band 66 has a downlink extension without a same-offset uplink channel. Band 70 supports different duplex-separation arrangements, so this tool does not guess its paired channel. Band 106 has a specific 3 MHz channel restriction. Band 103 and other narrow-band uses require the relevant NB-IoT rules; NB-IoT sub-raster offsets are outside this converter's scope.</p>
